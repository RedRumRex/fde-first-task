# Tone Guide: Build Fast with AI blog

> Built from the customer's own posts on [blog.buildfastwithai.com](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/), as they asked ("take a look at our other existing blogs"). Studied on 2026-10-26: the [Kimi K3.1 Pro review](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/kimi-k3-1-pro-review), the [Claude Duet review](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/claude-duet-review), [Nemotron 3 Ultra Explained](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/nemotron-3-ultra-explained) and the [SKY-001 review](https://blog.buildfastwithai.com/sky-001-review), plus the titles of the 13 most recent posts. The drafter reads this file on every run.

**Who reads it:** developers, tech leads and teams building real AI products ("developers, tech leaders, and organizations building real-world AI agents and applications"). They want to know what happened and whether it matters for what they build.

**Voice:** measured, practical and a little skeptical. The house posts take a launch seriously, then qualify it: *"Benchmarks are snapshots, not universal guarantees."* · *"The quality result is close, not a landslide."* · *"Do not judge Duet only by a polished demo."* Never hype.

## Rules

1. **Open with a plain definition and a date.** The first sentence says what the thing is or what happened, who did it and when, the way the house posts do: *"SKY-001 is an engineering world model announced by Skyfall AI on October 19, 2026."* Copy the date exactly as a source gives it.
2. **Start with a "Quick Answer" section.** The first section is headed `Quick Answer`: 4 sentences (about 70–90 words) that tell a busy reader the whole story before the details.
3. **Use numbered, Title Case headings, often phrased as questions.** After Quick Answer, write 2 numbered sections, e.g. `1. What Did Intel Agree To?` and `2. What It Means for Builders`. Each is 7 to 9 sentences (about 140–190 words) and uses facts from more than one source. Keep headings under 8 words.
4. **Talk to the reader as "you".** Companies and products are in the third person. Never write "we" or "I".
5. **Attribute every figure to whoever said it.** Use "Intel says", "according to Reuters", or "company-reported" for a vendor's own numbers. After a headline number, a short caveat in the house style is good ("That figure is company-reported."), as long as it adds no new fact.
6. **Mix medium sentences with short ones.** Most sentences are 15 to 25 words. A short line of 3 to 8 words adds emphasis after a long one ("That difference matters."). One idea per sentence.
7. **End with a "Bottom Line" section.** The last section is headed `Bottom Line`: 3 sentences (about 50–70 words). It may restate one cited fact, and its last sentence starts with "Bottom line:" and says specifically who should care and what to watch, using only facts already cited above: *"Bottom line: follow this if you build coding agents across large repositories."*
8. **Opinions add no new facts.** Analysis and caveats ("Benchmarks are signals, not guarantees.") are opinion and must not introduce numbers, dates, names or events that aren't already cited.
9. **Headline:** Title Case, a subject then a colon and a subtitle, under 12 words, no emoji. An ampersand is fine ("Intel Stake: Price, Shares & What Changes"). Add the event's year in parentheses only if a source states that year.
10. **No hype words or emphasis tricks.** No "revolutionary", "game-changing" or "unprecedented" (unless quoting a source), no exclamation marks, no emoji, no bold.
11. **When sources disagree, say so.** Give each figure with its source and don't quietly pick one.

**Length check:** count sentences, not words: 4 + 7 to 9 + 7 to 9 + 3 = at least 21 sentences, about 350 to 500 words. The checker rejects any post under 300 words.

## What this post does not copy from the house posts

The house posts run about 4,000 words with an FAQ, comparison images, ratings, "Recommended Blogs" and calls to action ("Book a 30-min call"). Our scope is a 350 to 500 word news post, so it keeps the voice and the frame (Quick Answer → numbered sections → Bottom Line) and leaves the FAQ, ratings and calls to action for the editor to add.
