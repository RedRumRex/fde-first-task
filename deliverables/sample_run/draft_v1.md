# US Government Takes 10% Stake in Intel

## Overview of the Stake Acquisition

On August 22, 2025, President Donald Trump announced that the U.S. government will acquire a 10% stake in Intel Corporation [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/). The government will receive 433.3 million shares at $20.47 each, totaling approximately $8.9 billion [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/) [[2]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). This stake will not grant the government a board seat but will allow it to vote on shareholder matters with some exceptions [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/).

## Context and Motivation

The acquisition is part of the CHIPS Act, which aims to bolster domestic semiconductor manufacturing [[2]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). Experts suggest that the government's involvement is driven by national security concerns and competition with China [[3]](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/06/nx-s1-5530190/government-stake-intel-ai-competition-china). Historically, government stakes in companies have typically occurred during economic crises, making this move unusual [[2]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel) [[3]](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/06/nx-s1-5530190/government-stake-intel-ai-competition-china).

## Reactions and Implications

The move has received mixed reactions, with some lawmakers supporting it while others express concern about government overreach [[2]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). Critics worry that government ownership could lead to favoritism and politicization in business operations [[3]](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/06/nx-s1-5530190/government-stake-intel-ai-competition-china). Supporters argue that the investment is necessary for national security and to revitalize the U.S. semiconductor industry [[2]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel).

## Why it matters

This stake in Intel marks a significant shift in U.S. government policy towards private industry, particularly in the tech sector. For builders and industry stakeholders, this could signal a new era of public-private partnerships in critical technologies. The long-term effects of this investment on market dynamics and innovation remain uncertain, raising questions about future government involvement.
