# U.S. Government Takes 10% Stake in Intel

## Deal Overview

President Donald Trump announced that the federal government will receive a 10% stake in Intel [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/) [[3]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). The government will acquire 433.3 million shares at $20.47 per share [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/) [[3]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). The transaction is valued at about $8.9 billion [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/). Nearly $6 billion of the purchase will be funded with previously awarded CHIPS Act grants that have not yet been paid [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/). For builders, the key point is that the government is using existing grant commitments rather than new cash. Intel will not grant the government any voting or board seats [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/). A warrant gives the White House an option to buy an additional 5% of shares within five years if Intel’s foundry business falls below 51% ownership [[1]](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/22/trump-says-us-government-will-take-10-stake-intel/).

## Context and Background

Intel received $8.5 billion in grants and $11 billion in loans from the Biden administration for new U.S. fabs [[3]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). The CHIPS and Science Act, signed in 2022, set aside $53 billion for semiconductor incentives [[3]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). The stake therefore links the financing program directly to an equity position, a departure from typical grant-only models. Intel’s market capitalization is just over $100 billion [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/). Intel reported a loss of nearly $19 billion last year after a $1.7 billion profit in 2023 [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/).

## Political Reaction

Senators Ron Wyden and Jeff Merkley criticized the deal as harmful to the semiconductor sector [[2]](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/22/intel-trump-us-government-stake/). Republican senators Thom Tillis and Rand Paul opposed the stake, while Senator Bernie Sanders expressed support [[3]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-economic-and-policy-experts-think-about-the-u-s-governments-stake-in-intel). The split highlights how the deal sits at the intersection of national‑security priorities and concerns about government overreach.

## Why it matters

For technology builders, the government's equity stake signals a new model of public‑private partnership that could shape future financing of critical chip capacity. If the warrant is exercised, the government's ownership could rise to roughly 15%, potentially influencing strategic decisions without formal voting rights. The arrangement also raises questions about precedent: will other strategic industries see similar equity injections, or will backlash curb such moves? Ultimately, the stake ties taxpayer‑funded incentives directly to corporate performance, creating both accountability and a new risk profile for public money.
